## 1) Japan Sticker Culture

**Lede:** In Japan, stickers rarely arrive alone — they arrive with washi, planners, and the quiet permission to decorate an ordinary week.

Japanese sticker culture sits inside a larger stationery ritual. The techo is not just a diary; it is a stage. Washi tape from brands like mt (Kamoi Kakoshi) taught a generation that industrial tape could become a craft medium — torn edges, layered patterns, soft colour fields. Stickers then enter as punctuation: a seal, a flake, a character, a seasonal motif.

What makes Japan distinct is the balance between restraint and play. A page can stay airy. A roll of washi can be the hero. Collecting is often about materials and artists — Cozyca collaborations, Mind Wave character sheets, boutique washi houses — and about the pleasure of returning to the same notebook across months.

For Season 01, Japan is the craft pole of Asia’s sticker map: collecting as careful composition.

## 2) Hong Kong Sticker Books

**Lede:** In Hong Kong, a sticker book can feel like a city archive you carry in your bag — tram, cha chaan teng, cats, and jokes that only locals clock.

Hong Kong sticker culture thrives at the meeting point of childhood collecting and indie character IP. Sticker books are treasure systems: pages waiting to be filled, sheets waiting to be chosen, a sense that finishing is optional and rearranging is the real game. Alongside mass-market sheets, local illustrators have built characters that stick to phones, laptops, and stationery — soft markers of belonging in a dense city.

Discovery often happens through Pinkoi shops, design fairs, and word-of-mouth among journaling friends. Artists such as Oh Mankee (HOKO) and Missquai show how local motifs become sticker-first merch, then grow into broader IP. Dodolulu’s watercolor sheets and matchbox flakes show another path: intimate illustration that travels internationally without losing a Hong Kong hand-feel.

Season 01 treats Hong Kong sticker books as memory technology — playful, local, and proudly unfinished.

**Draft length:** ~340 words  
**Sources:** Knowledge Hub HK indie IP · Artist DB (Oh Mankee, Missquai, dodolulu)

## 3) Korean Stationery Trends

**Lede:** Korea’s stationery wave is loud in the best way — dakku pages dense with flakes, polco frames dressed like mini stages, character stores that feel like theme parks for paper.

Dakku (다이어리 꾸미기) turned diary decorating into a youth craft language: stickers, masking tapes, washi, and decorative layers that celebrate ordinary days. Polco — decorating Polaroids and photo cards — pulled stickers into fandom and friendship economies. Retail surfaces like ARTBOX make discovery physical; character IP from Kakao Friends and LINE Friends keeps the digital-sticker origin story alive in lifestyle form.

Design stationery brands such as ICONIC, Appree, Paperian, and Dailylike expand the toolkit: cheerful planners, nature-detail stickers, clean decorative systems. Morning Glory’s illustration-fair culture shows how manufacturer + illustrator ecosystems keep sticker supply creative, not only corporate.

For Season 01, Korea is the high-energy pole: stickers as social craft, fandom-adjacent play, and retail theatre.

**Draft length:** ~330 words  
**Sources:** Knowledge Hub dakku / polco / Kakao · Brand DB (ARTBOX, Kakao Friends, LINE Friends + Batch 002 KR brands)

## 4) Taiwan Creative Journaling

**Lede:** Taiwan’s journaling scene feels like a design export in progress — PET tape with collage density, kiss-cut stickers with artist signatures, and brands that treat paper as a portable gallery.

Taiwanese stationery culture is illustrator-forward. Brands and artist lines such as Loidesign, OURS, Pion, Some Sort of Fern, and Chamil Garden show how drawing practice becomes product language: floral collage, watercolor forests, stamp systems, and PET tapes that travel through Pinkoi and international stockists. Creative Expo booths turn these worlds into walkable discovery.

The journaling habit here is often layered and graphic — less minimal techo air, more collage authorship. Collectors chase artist identity as much as motif. That makes Taiwan a critical Season 01 node for Artist Discovery and Brand Discovery: sticker culture as indie design industry.

**Draft length:** ~300 words  
**Sources:** Knowledge Hub Taiwan PET / collage / Pinkoi · Artist + Brand DB TW rows
